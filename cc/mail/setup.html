Connecting your accounts

Google and Microsoft only let a mail program into your account if it has its own “keys.” Big apps like Outlook have theirs built in. Mailroom runs on your computer just for you, so you make your own keys once. They’re free, and Mailroom keeps them on your computer.

Each setup takes about 10 minutes. You only do it once per provider. After that, you can connect as many Gmail or Outlook addresses as you like.

Gmail

Have Mailroom open in another tab. You’ll copy two keys from Google into Settings and accounts.

1. Create a project

  1. Go to console.cloud.google.com and sign in with your Gmail account. Accept the terms if Google asks.
  2. At the top left, click the project picker (it may say “Select a project”), then New project.
  3. Name it Mailroom and click Create. When it finishes, make sure Mailroom is the selected project.

2. Turn on Gmail access

  1. Open the Gmail API page.
  2. Click Enable.

3. Describe your app

  1. Open Google Auth Platform and click Get started.
  2. App name: Mailroom. User support email: your Gmail address. Click Next.
  3. Audience: choose External. Click Next.
  4. Contact information: your Gmail address. Click Next, tick the agreement, then Create.

4. Add yourself as a tester

  1. In the left menu, open Audience.
  2. Under Test users, click Add users and enter every Gmail address you plan to connect. Click Save.

5. Make the keys

  1. In the left menu, open Clients, then Create client.
  2. Application type: Web application. Name: Mailroom.
  3. Under Authorized redirect URIs, click Add URI and paste exactly:
    http://localhost:4777/auth/google/callback
  4. Click Create. Google shows a Client ID and a Client secret. Copy both now, or click Download JSON. Google may not show the secret again.

6. Connect

  1. In Mailroom, open Settings and accounts. Under Gmail, paste the Client ID and Client secret, then click Save keys.
  2. Click Connect a Gmail account and sign in.
  3. Google warns that it hasn’t verified this app. That’s expected, because you made the app yourself. Click Continue.
  4. On the permissions screen, tick every box and click Continue. Your inbox appears in Mailroom.

Gmail asks you to reconnect about once a week. Google does this for apps in “testing.” When it happens, Mailroom marks the account with a !. Click it, or click Reconnect in Settings, and sign in again.

Optional: stop the weekly sign-in. Google only lets you publish after the Branding page has three links on a website you own: a home page, a privacy policy and terms of service. Your website’s domain (for example yourcompany.com) must also be listed under Authorized domains. Google doesn’t accept localhost. Until those are in, Publish app stays grayed out.

Once they’re saved, open Audience and click Publish app. You’ll still see the “hasn’t verified this app” warning when you connect, and only 100 people can ever use it.

If you don’t have a website, stay in Testing. Reconnecting once a week takes a few seconds.

Outlook, Hotmail and Microsoft 365

Before you start

Microsoft only lets you make keys inside a “directory” (also called a tenant). Work and school accounts already have one. Personal Outlook.com and Hotmail accounts might not.

  • Personal account: sign in at the link in step 1. If Microsoft says you don’t have access or a directory, create a free Azure account at azure.microsoft.com/free with the same Microsoft account, then come back. Microsoft asks for a card to confirm who you are. Making keys is free.
  • Work or school account: your IT department may block app registration or ask to approve the app. If you see “Need admin approval,” send them this guide.

1. Register the app

  1. Go to App registrations in Microsoft Entra and sign in.
  2. Click New registration.
  3. Name: Mailroom.
  4. Supported account types: choose Accounts in any organizational directory and personal Microsoft accounts. This lets you connect Outlook.com, Hotmail and work addresses.
  5. Under Redirect URI, pick Web and paste exactly:
    http://localhost:4777/auth/microsoft/callback
  6. Click Register.
  7. On the page that opens, copy the Application (client) ID. That’s your Client ID.

2. Make a secret

  1. In the left menu, open Certificates & secrets, then New client secret.
  2. Description: Mailroom. For Expires, choose the longest option offered.
  3. Click Add, then copy the Value right away. Don’t copy the Secret ID. Microsoft hides the value once you leave the page.

Write down the expiry date. When it passes, Outlook accounts stop working until you make a new secret and paste it into Mailroom under Change keys.

3. Allow mail access

  1. In the left menu, open API permissions, then Add a permission, then Microsoft Graph, then Delegated permissions.
  2. Search for and tick Mail.ReadWrite, Mail.Send, offline_access and User.Read. Click Add permissions.

4. Connect

  1. In Mailroom, open Settings and accounts. Under Outlook, paste the Client ID and the secret Value, then click Save keys.
  2. Click Connect an Outlook account, sign in, and click Accept.

If something goes wrong

“redirect_uri_mismatch” or “The redirect URI … does not match”
The redirect address in Google or Microsoft has to match exactly: http (not https), localhost, port 4777, and no slash at the end.
“Access blocked: Mailroom has not completed the Google verification process”
Add your address under Audience, then Test users, in Google Auth Platform.
“invalid_client” or “Invalid client secret”
The secret was copied wrong. For Microsoft, copy the Value, not the Secret ID. Paste it again under Change keys.
An account shows a red !
Its sign-in expired. Click it and sign in again. Your settings and signature are kept.
Mailroom won’t open
Make sure the black Control Center window is still open. If you closed it, double-click the start file again.
